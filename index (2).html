    Chapter 012020Hyderabad, India

    The year the shelves went empty.

    I was studying Digital Marketing & E-Commerce when COVID-19 hit. Hospitals ran short of masks and gloves. Factories shut down and parts stopped arriving. A shortage of semiconductors held up cars and laptops on the other side of the world.

    Every empty shelf had a long chain of decisions behind it: who supplied what, how much was kept in stock, which ship got which container. I wanted to understand that chain, and I wanted to be one of the people who could make it work better.

    Marketing asked what people wanted. Supply chain asked how it would actually reach them. I found the second question more interesting.

    Chapter 02May 2023 – May 2024Hyderabad, IndiaInternship

    Four districts, forty dealers, and a change of plans.

    My internship at Nichino India was on paper a sales and marketing role. In practice it was my first look at a distribution network. I coordinated dealers and regional promotions for the TYCHI crop protection line across four districts, and ran product demonstrations for farmers and dealers.

    The useful part was what came back. Dealers gave me feedback on pricing, and I carried it back to the sales team. It showed me how much a product's success depends on what happens between the factory and the field.

    Then I took a supply chain course at Woxsen University and the plan changed. I finished my BBA in May 2024 and applied to study supply chain full time.

    Chapter 03Jan – May 2025Houston, TX · Gulf of MexicoCompleted

    Seven routes, two vessels, and one better network.

    Offshore Routing Optimization · Coastal logistics case project

    I landed in Houston in January 2025 and started the M.S. in Supply Chain Management at the C.T. Bauer College of Business. Houston runs on energy, so my first big project fit the city: three Gulf energy operators spending $1.39M a year moving supplies to offshore platforms.

    The question was whether the same platforms could be served with fewer, fuller trips. Using the Clarke–Wright Savings Algorithm, we merged stops that made sense together and rebuilt the network. Seven routes became five, served by two vessels.

    Chapter 04Aug – Dec 2025UH Bauer · SAP S/4HANACompleted

    Running a muesli company from inside SAP.

    SAP S/4HANA ERP Simulation · Analytics & Enterprise Operations

    That fall our team ran a simulated cereal manufacturer end to end in SAP S/4HANA. Every decision went through the system the way it would at a real company.

    I worked the procure-to-pay cycle: purchase orders, goods receipt, invoice matching, and keeping vendor, material and bill-of-materials data clean. I ran demand planning and MRP so production had the right ingredients at the right time without stockpiling. We tracked margins, return on assets and working capital in Power BI scorecards and used them to adjust pricing and product mix.

    Chapter 05Aug 2025 – presentUH Bauer · Decision & Information SciencesCurrent

    Teaching the models I was learning to build.

    That same August I became an Instructional Assistant. Three semesters of Business Modeling & Decision Analysis later, and now in Supply Chain Analytics, I have helped more than 350 students build and fix their models.

    Most of the job is detective work. A student's Solver model returns something strange, and we trace it back to a missing constraint or a wrong assumption. Then comes the part I enjoy most: explaining what the answer means for the business, in plain words.

    If I can't explain why the model picked an answer, I don't really understand the model yet.

    Chapter 06Jan – May 2026Strategic Supply ManagementVerified certificate

    Six rounds of supplier decisions, and a company back in the black.

    The Fresh Connection · Strategic sourcing & supplier management

    We took over a juice company that was losing money. My job was the supply side. Each round I compared suppliers on quoted unit price, lead time and reject rate. The cheapest quote often turned out to be the expensive one once rejected components were counted, so I paid for supplier quality upgrades that cut those rejection costs across the product line.

    On the operations side, we split one undifferentiated supply chain into four customer channels and sped up production with SMED changeovers, faster line speeds and preventive maintenance.

    Chapter 07Jun – Jul 2026UH Humana Integrated Health Sciences InstitutePresented

    Back to where it started: the medicine cabinet.

    Value-Based Healthcare Analyst · SHERP 2026

    The shortages that pulled me into supply chain started in healthcare, so this project felt like coming full circle. Patients were getting duplicate prescriptions and falling into refill gaps, which costs health plans money and puts people at risk.

    I cleaned, merged and validated more than 7,000 pre- and post-survey records in SQL, then built the financial model for a 100,000-member population. We worked with physicians and pharmacists on a medication safety solution and presented it as an interdisciplinary team.

    Chapter 082026St. Louis, MO · CFHA Annual ConferencePoster presented

    From survey data to a conference floor.

    Research poster · Collaborative Family Healthcare Association (CFHA) 2026

    Our research on value-based care education went to the 2026 CFHA Annual Conference in St. Louis, and I presented the poster with the team. It was titled “Evaluating the Impact of a Specialized Value-Based Care Course on Self-Reported Knowledge and Attitudinal Change Toward VBC.”

    The study looked at how structured value-based care education can build understanding of VBC and readiness for it across healthcare stakeholders. At the poster I talked through the findings with clinicians, researchers and healthcare leaders. Those conversations covered integrated care, the hard parts of implementation, and where healthcare delivery is heading.

    Coming from supply chain and operations, I left with a sharper interest in where healthcare operations, analytics and patient-centered care meet.

    With Dr. Dave Curtis, Dr. Sara Elkins, Dr. Ken Janda and the team at the Humana Institute, Tilman J. Fertitta Family College of Medicine, University of Houston.
